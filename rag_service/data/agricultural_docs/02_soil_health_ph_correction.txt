## CHAPTER 1: UNDERSTANDING SOIL pH

### 1.1 What is Soil pH?
Soil pH is the measure of hydrogen ion (H+) concentration in soil solution:
- pH < 7.0: Acidic
- pH = 7.0: Neutral
- pH > 7.0: Alkaline (Basic)

Optimal pH range for most Indian food crops: 6.0 to 7.5

### 1.2 Effect of pH on Nutrient Availability
pH 4-5: Severe toxicity of Al3+, Mn2+ and Fe3+. Phosphorus heavily fixed. Molybdenum unavailable.
pH 5-6 (Acidic): Phosphorus partially fixed. Ca, Mg deficiency possible. Nitrogen bacteria activity low.
pH 6-7 (Slightly Acidic - Optimal): Most macronutrients and micronutrients maximally available.
pH 7-8 (Neutral-Slightly Alkaline): Iron, Zinc, Manganese availability begins to decline.
pH 8-9 (Strongly Alkaline): Iron, Zinc, Copper, Boron deficiency. Phosphorus fixed with calcium.
pH > 9 (Sodic/Saline): Structural collapse of soil, plant growth severely inhibited.

### 1.3 Crop Tolerance to pH

Crop — Optimal pH Range:
- Wheat: 6.0-7.5
- Rice/Paddy: 5.5-7.0 (Paddy grows in flooded, anaerobic conditions)
- Maize: 5.8-7.0
- Cotton: 6.0-8.0
- Sugarcane: 6.0-7.5
- Tomato: 5.5-7.0
- Potato: 5.0-6.0 (slightly acidic preferred)
- Soybean: 6.0-7.0
- Groundnut: 6.0-7.0

## CHAPTER 2: ACIDIC SOILS (pH < 6.0)

### 2.1 Distribution
Acidic soils cover approximately 49 million hectares in India, concentrated in:
- North East India (Assam, Meghalaya, Arunachal Pradesh)
- Eastern India (West Bengal, Jharkhand, Odisha)
- Parts of Kerala, Tamil Nadu
- Laterite soils of Western Ghats

### 2.2 Causes of Soil Acidity
1. High rainfall: leaches basic cations (Ca2+, Mg2+, K+, Na+) from topsoil
2. Organic matter decomposition: releases organic acids
3. Nitrification of ammoniacal fertilizers: produces H+ ions
4. Mining and industrial activity
5. Continuous use of acidifying fertilizers (Ammonium Sulfate)

### 2.3 Problems in Acidic Soils
- Aluminum toxicity: Al3+ solubilizes at pH < 5.5, inhibits root elongation, phosphorus uptake
- Manganese toxicity: Mn2+ toxic at pH < 5.5
- Phosphorus fixation: P fixed as AlPO4 and FePO4
- Low calcium, magnesium availability
- Reduced microbial activity (nitrogen-fixing bacteria need pH > 5.5)

### 2.4 CORRECTION: Agricultural Lime Application

Agricultural Lime (Calcium Carbonate, CaCO3) — Liming
Reaction: CaCO3 + 2H+ → Ca2+ + H2O + CO2

Liming neutralizes soil acidity by replacing H+ and Al3+ on exchange sites with Ca2+.

**Dosage Guide (Agricultural Lime / CaCO3):**
| Current pH | Target pH | Lime Dose (Sandy) | Lime Dose (Clay) |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| 4.5 → 6.0 | 6.0 | 400-600 kg/acre | 700-1000 kg/acre |
| 5.0 → 6.0 | 6.0 | 250-400 kg/acre | 500-700 kg/acre |
| 5.5 → 6.5 | 6.5 | 200-300 kg/acre | 400-600 kg/acre |

Standard General Recommendation: 250-300 kg Agricultural Lime per acre (617-741 kg/ha) for soils at pH 5.0-5.5.

**Application Method:**
1. Apply lime 3-4 weeks BEFORE sowing
2. Broadcast evenly across the field
3. Incorporate into soil by ploughing to 15 cm depth
4. Allow reaction time before seeding (lime takes 2-4 weeks to fully react)
5. Re-test soil after 6 months to evaluate pH change

**Expected pH change:** 0.5-1.0 pH units per application of 200-300 kg lime/acre

### 2.5 Quick-acting Alternatives
- Burnt lime (Calcium Oxide, CaO): 56% Ca, faster acting, use at 50% the rate of CaCO3
- Dolomitic lime (CaCO3 + MgCO3): preferred when Mg deficiency also present
- Fly ash: 250-500 kg/acre — has liming value due to calcium content (use with soil test)

## CHAPTER 3: ALKALINE SOILS (pH > 7.5)

### 3.1 Types of Problem Soils
a) Calcareous Soils (pH 7.5-8.5): High free lime (CaCO3) content. Common in arid/semi-arid regions.
b) Sodic/Usar Soils (pH 8.5-10+): High exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP > 15%). Black alkali soils. Common in Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Punjab, Rajasthan.

### 3.2 Problems in Alkaline/Sodic Soils
- Iron (Fe), Zinc (Zn), Manganese (Mn) deficiency (precipitate at high pH)
- Phosphorus fixation with calcium
- Sodium-induced clay dispersion → poor soil structure, surface crusting
- Compaction and waterlogging
- High osmotic stress on plants

### 3.3 CORRECTION: Gypsum Application

Gypsum (Calcium Sulfate, CaSO4·2H2O) — Primary Reclamation Amendment for Sodic Soils
Reaction: 2NaX + CaSO4 → CaX2 + Na2SO4
(X = soil exchange site)

Sodium is replaced by calcium on exchange sites. Na2SO4 is soluble and can be leached out.

**Gypsum Dosage:**
| ESP Level | Gypsum Dose |
|-----------|------------|
| Low sodicity (ESP 15-25) | 300-500 kg/acre |
| Medium sodicity (ESP 25-40) | 500-750 kg/acre |
| High sodicity (ESP > 40) | 750-1000 kg/acre |

Standard Recommendation: 500 kg Gypsum per acre (1235 kg/ha) for moderately sodic soils.

**Application Method:**
1. Broadcast gypsum across field
2. Incorporate to 15 cm depth
3. Apply heavy irrigation (250-300 mm) to flush displaced sodium below root zone
4. Allow 30 days before seeding
5. Re-test after 1 season

### 3.4 Additional Amendments for Alkaline Soils
- Elemental Sulfur (S): Oxidizes to H2SO4, reducing pH. Use 200-400 kg/acre. Slow acting (3-6 months).
- Iron Sulfate (FeSO4): Rapid pH reduction, provides iron. 10-15 kg/acre.
- Organic manures: FYM at 5-10 tonnes/ha improves structure and provides chelated micronutrients.
- Pyrite (FeS2): 250-500 kg/acre. Slow-acting but effective.

### 3.5 Micronutrient Management in Alkaline Soils
Iron deficiency (Lime-induced Chlorosis):
- Symptoms: Yellowing between veins (interveinal chlorosis) on young leaves — opposite to N deficiency which starts from old leaves
- Correction: FeSO4 spray (1%) on leaves OR soil application of chelated EDTA-Fe at 5-10 kg/ha

Zinc deficiency:
- Common in flooded rice, maize, cotton in alkaline soils
- Symptoms: Bronze discoloration in rice, white bud in maize
- Correction: ZnSO4 at 25 kg/ha soil application OR 0.5% spray

## CHAPTER 4: SOIL ORGANIC CARBON (SOC)

### 4.1 Importance
- Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) improves soil structure, water retention, and nutrient-holding capacity
- India's average SOC is critically low: 0.4-0.5% (target >0.5%)
- SOC decline due to: reduced organic inputs, intensive tillage, burning of residues

### 4.2 Building SOC
- Farmyard Manure (FYM): 5-10 tonnes/ha annually
- Compost: 2-5 tonnes/ha (enriched with Trichoderma for disease suppression)
- Green manure crops: Dhaincha (Sesbania), Sunn hemp — incorporate at flowering
- Crop residue retention: Avoid burning; incorporate rice/wheat straw
- Vermicompost: 2-4 tonnes/ha
